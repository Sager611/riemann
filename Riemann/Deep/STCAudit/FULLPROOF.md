## C. Mutual consistency and the difference of methods
- No conflict: both are upper bounds for the same |S(T)|. CG's envelope (2.2) lies below Ismail's nine-row minimum at every T ≥ e, by 0.472 at T = e, 1.36 at T0 = 3.06·10¹⁰ (3.899 vs 5.256), 1.52 at 3·10¹² (4.395 vs 5.914), 9.96 at 10²⁰⁰ (compare_envelopes.py).
- Why the leading constants differ (Ismail C1 ≥ 0.105, CG 0.0838): Backlund's method bounds the zeros of f_N(s) = ½(ζ(s+iT)^N + ζ(s−iT)^N) in a Jensen disc; its log T coefficient is C̄1/(2π log(r/(c − ½))), an average over the circle of the growth exponents of ζ (convexity/sub-Weyl on Re s ≤ 1), bounded below by ≈ 0.1 with present inputs (BW: 0.10076 nearly optimal; CG [7]: ≥ Λ(0) = 0.0786 for discs near 1, even under Lindelöf). The Turing-type method uses no bound for ζ inside the strip: N(T) is exactly (1/π)(2 arg ξ(s1) − arg ξ(s2)) plus a zero sum of f, f is majorised by U = Σ c_ι G_ι whose zero sums are (ξ'/ξ)^{(j−1)}(s1) with Re s1 = ½ + d > 1, so log T enters only through (c_{R,1}/2)ψ(s1/2), giving C1 = c_{R,1}/(2π), minimised by linear programming under (K). Different extremal problems, no inconsistency; CG's numbers also beat Bellotti–Wong 2026's Turing-type 0.0946 by using 19 instead of ≤ 4 coefficients (their Remark 2.5 limit ≈ 0.079).
