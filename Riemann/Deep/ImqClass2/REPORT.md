## Proof (three ranges)

1. d ≥ e^27.36 (< 7.627e11): h(−d) > 10000 (Littlewood78, Cor. 2, evaluated by threshold.py with the certified cells of
   Littlewood78/cert.py, unchanged, sha256 009ce50f…; rerun 6 Nov 2026 for N = 6000, 7000, 7500, …, 10^4: thresholds.txt,
   the same values as ImqClass where both exist).
2. 2.8·10^10 ≤ d ≤ 762,700,000,000: the sieve isieve2.c (next section) certified M(d) ≥ 10^4 + 1 for every candidate d
   (d ≡ 3, 7, 11, 15, 4, 8 mod 16, fundamental or not; 275,512,500,000 candidates in 38 chunks), except 177 lanes
   left for the exact tier 3, of which 144 have M(d) ≥ 10^4 + 1 and 33 are fundamental with M(d) ≤ 10^4 (tier 4,
   all in [2.8·10^10, 3.1·10^10)); the unconditional tier-4 tool t4.gp (classno_upto of 2610.21511: the subgroup generated by
   the prime forms of norm ≤ √(d/3)) gives h > 10^4 for 32 of them and h = 9819 for d = 28,154,696,707.
   Since h(−d) ≥ M(d) for fundamental −d, the only field with h ≤ 10^4 in this range is d = 28,154,696,707 (h = 9819),
   which is also in the tables.
3. d < 29,796,335,616: the Mosunov–Jacobson tables (Math. Comp. 85 (2016); unconditional, verified by the authors with the
   Eichler–Selberg trace formula), LMFDB files cl{3,7}mod8.k.gz and cl{4,8}mod16.k.gz, k = 0..110, extracted by mjx with
   all its checks (9,056,999,962 entries; SHA-256 and size of every file in lmfdb_files.log).
The ranges cover every d (2.8·10^10 < 29,796,335,616; the sieve reaches 762,700,000,000 ≥ e^27.36). For N < 10000 the same ranges work.

## The sieve change (isieve2.c; isieve.c of ImqClass is kept unchanged as the fallback and as the cross-check)

- Same bound family (Watkins' minima): for fundamental −d, h(−d) ≥ M(d) = Σ_{a ≤ X} r(a), X = ⌊√(d/4)⌋, r multiplicative
  (2 at split prime powers, 0 at inert primes, 1 at a ramified prime, 0 at its square). Classes c mod 240240 fix the
  primes ≤ 13; G_c and F_c(t) = Σ_{m ∈ G_c, m ≤ t} r(m) as in ImqClass. Every a = m·n (m ∈ G_c, n built from primes > 13)
  has r(a) = r(m)r(n), so with X_s = X(segment start) ≤ X(d)
  M(d) ≥ F_c(X_s) + Σ_{17 ≤ p ≤ X_s/17, p split} 2Σ_{k≥1} F_c(X_s/p^k) + Σ_{17 ≤ p < q, pq ≤ X_s, p, q split} 4F_c(X_s/(pq))
         + Σ_{X_s/17 < p ≤ min(X_s, 250000), p split} 2F_c(X_s/p).
  New against isieve.c: the pairs n = pq (the m·p·q terms of the brief), the prime powers p^k, and a finer rounding of
  the weights (down to 2^k or 3·2^{k−1}, at least 2/3 of the exact weight; isieve.c rounded to a power of 2, at least
  1/2). All weights are integers, rounded down and capped at 32767, so the sum stays a lower bound; additions and the
  final comparison with N + 1 are exact (16 bit-sliced planes; a carry out of the top plane means a sum ≥ 2^16 > N + 1).
- Kernel: lanes are d = c + 240240·j for consecutive j; groups of 8 words (512 lanes, one 512-bit vector per bit plane)
  of one class run through phase A (primes ≤ X_s/17; their split bits are kept for the pairs), the pairs, and phase B,
  until all lanes of the group are certified. Phases A and B are prime-major over the live groups of a class (each
  prime's bit table is read as one stream), the pairs per group, counting with Harley–Seal carry-save adders in runs of
  equal weight. This removes isieve.c's cost of sweeping finished words (it went over all 128 words of a class for
  every batch of primes until the class's hardest word was done).
- Tier 3 (exact M(d) capped at N + 1, depth-first) takes the Kronecker symbols from the bit tables (checked against
  Jacobi symbols at 400,000 random (d, p) at the start of every run, and every tier-3 value is recomputed with Jacobi
  symbols in test mode); tier 4 (fundamental d with M(d) ≤ N) as in ImqClass (t4.gp = classno_upto of 2610.21511).
- Speed at N = 10^4 (one Apple M4 efficiency core, machine shared with other agents): isieve.c needed 215 ns per
  candidate at 6·10^10 and at 10^11 (windows of 5·10^8 in d), isieve2 97 ns at 6·10^10 and 40–55 ns above 10^11 (run log
  below). On ImqClass's production-size window W1 (1.53·10^10) the new bound leaves 586 lanes for tier 3 where isieve.c
  left 52,585; on its N = 2·10^4 window at 2·10^10, 25,886 where isieve.c left 133,555.

## Caveats

- The analytic step (range 1) is Littlewood78, a reviewed paper proof with arb-ball certificates, not Lean-checked.
- The comparison with the GRH counts of HJKMP and the PARI checks are consistency checks, not inputs of the proof.
- A second verdict-only reviewer pass on the theorem and its three ranges (07:28): coverage, endpoints, small d, the
  non-fundamental candidates and the one field in the sieve range are all OK; the argument is correct given the
  stated inputs. Its one objection was the record wording; the "Record status" paragraph above answers it.
- The tables are trusted as published (Mosunov–Jacobson verified them with the Eichler–Selberg trace formula); our checks
  cover format, completeness of the discriminant lists per file, internal consistency, ImqClass's SHA-256 rows, the
  lists of 2610.21511 (through ImqClass) and the GRH counts of HJKMP.
- isieve2.c had a verdict-only reviewer pass (bound and weights, bit-sliced exactness, lane-to-bit mapping, accounting:
  SOUND) and the tests in tests.txt; it is not formally verified. The test sampling selects multiples of K (so for even
  K only even d; repeated with odd K), and its tested lanes always have K's primes ramified.
- Process rule: the sieve was the only compute process; the downloader (curl plus mjx bursts at nice 10) ran beside it
  throughout, as in ImqClass. The machine was shared with other agents (load average 5–11), so times are wall-clock
  under load.

- N = 2·10^4 would need the tables to about 1.2·10^11 (450 slabs) and a sieve on a range 3–4 times longer with larger
  X (about 8 times the work at the same speed); a stronger bound for the hard classes (or an analytic threshold below
  e^27.36) is the lever.
