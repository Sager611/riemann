SEEN 2611.12438 2026-11-09 Nontrivial Zeros of the Riemann Zeta Function and Its Derivative
   The nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function lie at the heart of analytic number theory. Through the explicit formulas, they govern the distribution of prime numbers: the closer all nontrivial zeros lie to the critical line $\Re(s)=\frac12$, the more uniformly the primes are distributed. The Riemann Hypothesis-asserting that all nontrivial zeros satisfy $\Re(s)=\frac12$-is equivalent to the optimal error bound $O(\sqrt{x}\log x)$ in the Prime Number Theorem. Beyond number theory, the zeros of $ζ(s)$ and its derivative $ζ'(s)$ are deeply intertwined: the horizontal distribution of $ζ'$-zeros near the critical line encodes information about the spacing of consecutive $ζ$-zeros. This paper establishes some results concerning the nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function.
