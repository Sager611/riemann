## 1. Orders and elements

**Lemma 1 (orders containing an element).** Let K/F be quadratic, 𝔭 a prime of F, π := π_𝔭, O_{K,𝔭} := O_K ⊗ O_𝔭 and O_{𝔭,f} := O_𝔭 + π^fO_{K,𝔭} (f ≥ 0).
- (a) Every O_𝔭-order of K_𝔭 is O_{𝔭,f} for a unique f, and disc O_{𝔭,f} = π^{2f}𝔡O_𝔭. Hence O_𝔣 ⊗ O_𝔭 = O_{𝔭,v_𝔭(𝔣)}, disc O_𝔣 = 𝔣²𝔡, and the O_𝔣 are
  pairwise distinct.
- (b) Let α ∈ K∖F with t := Tr α, μ := Nα in O_𝔭. Then α ∈ O_{𝔭,f} iff C_f(t,μ): some a ∈ O_𝔭 has π^f | t − 2a and π^{2f} | a² − ta + μ. C_f depends only
  on t mod 𝔭^{2f} and μ mod 𝔭^{2f}, and C_f(t,μ) ⟺ C_f(λt,λ²μ) for λ ∈ O_𝔭^×. C_f implies 𝔭^{2f} | t² − 4μ, and for 𝔭 ∤ 2 the converse holds.
- (c) With f_𝔭(α) := max{f : C_f(t,μ)}: O_𝔭[α] = O_{𝔭,f_𝔭(α)}, so v_𝔭(t² − 4μ) = 2f_𝔭(α) + v_𝔭(𝔡). For odd 𝔭: f_𝔭(α) = ⌊v_𝔭(t²−4μ)/2⌋ and
  η_K(𝔭) = ((t²−4μ)/𝔭)_* (= 0 iff v_𝔭(t² − 4μ) is odd iff 𝔭 ramifies).
- (d) Let 𝔩 be an integral ideal of F, α ∈ K∖F. Then α ∈ 𝔩O_𝔣 iff t ∈ 𝔩, μ ∈ 𝔩² and C_{v_𝔭(𝔣)}(t/π^{ℓ_𝔭}, μ/π^{2ℓ_𝔭}) for every 𝔭 (ℓ_𝔭 := v_𝔭(𝔩)). So
  {𝔣 : α ∈ 𝔩O_𝔣} = {𝔣 : 𝔣 | 𝔣(α;𝔩)}, 𝔣(α;𝔩) := Π_𝔭 𝔭^{f_𝔭(α/π^{ℓ_𝔭})}, and (t² − 4μ)O_F = 𝔩²𝔣(α;𝔩)²𝔡.

*Proof.* (a) O_{K,𝔭} is free over the DVR O_𝔭 with a basis 1, ω. An order R contains O_𝔭, and R/O_𝔭 is a nonzero submodule of O_{K,𝔭}/O_𝔭 ≅ O_𝔭ω, so
R = O_𝔭 + π^fωO_𝔭 = O_{𝔭,f}; the basis 1, π^fω gives the discriminant; the O_{𝔭,f} decrease strictly in f. The global statements follow since
an O_F-lattice is the intersection of its completions. (b) α ∈ O_{𝔭,f} iff (α − a)/π^f ∈ O_{K,𝔭} for some a ∈ O_𝔭, iff its trace (t − 2a)/π^f and norm
(a² − ta + μ)/π^{2f} are integral. The same a works for (t + π^{2f}d, μ + π^{2f}e), and λa works for (λt, λ²μ). The discriminant of the integral element
(α − a)/π^f is (t² − 4μ)/π^{2f}. For odd 𝔭 take a = t/2. (c) O_𝔭[α] = O_{𝔭,f'} by (a), and α ∈ O_{𝔭,f} ⟺ O_𝔭[α] ⊆ O_{𝔭,f} ⟺ f ≤ f'; disc O_𝔭[α] =
(t² − 4μ). For odd 𝔭, v_𝔭(𝔡) ∈ {0,1}, and K_𝔭 = F_𝔭(√(t²−4μ)) splits iff the unit part of t² − 4μ is a square. (d) α ∈ 𝔩O_𝔣 iff α/π^{ℓ_𝔭} ∈ O_{𝔭,v_𝔭(𝔣)} at every 𝔭;
apply (b) to α/π^{ℓ_𝔭}, whose trace and norm are t/π^ℓ and μ/π^{2ℓ}, and (c). □

**Lemma 2 (elementary facts).** Let K/F be CM and 𝔣 an integral ideal of F.
- (a) An integral ideal 𝔟 ∈ P_{K,O_F}(𝔣) is αO_K with α ∈ O_𝔣 (and αO_K prime to 𝔣); two such generators differ by a unit of O_𝔣.
- (b) For λ ∈ F^× with λO_F prime to 𝔣, λO_K ∈ P_{K,O_F}(𝔣). Hence for integral 𝔫 prime to 𝔣 the class j_𝔣(𝔫) := [𝔫O_K] ∈ G_{K,𝔣} depends only on the class of 𝔫
  in Cl_F; and for 𝔞 ∈ I_K(𝔣), [𝔞]^{−1} = [𝔞ᶜ]·j_𝔣(N_{K/F}𝔞)^{−1}.
- (c) If 𝔭 | 𝔣 then no α ∈ O_𝔣 has v_𝔭(N_{K/F}α) = 1, and {α ∈ O_𝔣 : 𝔭² | N_{K/F}α} = 𝔭O_{𝔣𝔭^{−1}} locally at 𝔭 (unchanged elsewhere).
- (d) |G_{K,𝔣}| ≤ h_K N𝔣 Π_{𝔭|𝔣}(1 + N𝔭^{−1}).
- (e) For α ∈ O_K, αO_K is prime to 𝔣 iff N_{K/F}(α)O_F is prime to 𝔣.

*Proof.* (a) Let 𝔟 = (α₁/α₂)O_K with α_i ∈ O_𝔣 prime to 𝔣 and α := α₁/α₂ ∈ O_K. Write α_i ≡ a_i mod 𝔣O_K, a_i ∈ O_F prime to 𝔣, and pick b ∈ O_F with
a₂b ≡ 1 mod 𝔣. Since α(α₂ − a₂) ∈ 𝔣O_K, αa₂ ≡ a₁ and α ≡ αa₂b ≡ a₁b mod 𝔣O_K: α ∈ O_𝔣. The same argument applied to a quotient of two generators shows
that it lies in O_𝔣. (b) Write λO_F = 𝔞𝔟^{−1} with coprime integral 𝔞, 𝔟, both prime to 𝔣. By the Chinese remainder theorem pick a ∈ 𝔟 with
v_𝔭(a) = 0 for all 𝔭 | 𝔣; then a, aλ ∈ O_F are prime to 𝔣 (aλ ∈ 𝔞𝔟^{−1}𝔟 ⊆ O_F), and λO_K = (aλ)O_K(aO_K)^{−1} with a, aλ ∈ O_F ⊂ O_𝔣. For 𝔫, 𝔫' in one class, 𝔫' = λ𝔫 with λO_F prime to 𝔣. Finally 𝔞𝔞ᶜ = N_{K/F}(𝔞)O_K. (c) Locally α = a + π^fβ (f ≥ 1, a ∈ O_𝔭,
β ∈ O_{K,𝔭}), Nα = a² + aπ^fTrβ + π^{2f}Nβ: if π ∤ a, Nα is a unit; if π | a, π² | Nα. And α ∈ πO_𝔭 + π^fO_{K,𝔭} = π O_{𝔭,f−1}. (d) Every class of Cl_K has an
ideal prime to 𝔣, so G_{K,𝔣} → Cl_K is onto with kernel P_K(𝔣)/P_{K,O_F}(𝔣) (principal ideals prime to 𝔣), a quotient of (O_K/𝔣O_K)^×/(O_F/𝔣)^×.
(e) A prime 𝔓 | 𝔭 divides α iff 𝔭 | Nα for one of the primes above 𝔭. □

## 2. The dual identity

**Lemma 3 (Hecke relations).** In Z[G_{K,𝔣}], for coprime 𝔞, 𝔟: T_{𝔞𝔟} = T_𝔞T_𝔟; for 𝔭 ∤ 𝔣: T_𝔭² = T_{𝔭²} + η(𝔭)j_𝔣(𝔭); for 𝔭 | 𝔣: T_𝔭 = T_{𝔭²} = 0.
Hence for squarefree 𝔫 = 𝔤𝔪, 𝔫' = 𝔤𝔪' with 𝔤 = (𝔫,𝔫'): T_𝔫T_{𝔫'} = Σ_{𝔠|𝔤}η_𝔣(𝔠)j_𝔣(𝔠)T_{𝔪𝔪'(𝔤/𝔠)²}.

*Proof.* As [R3] Lemma 1 with [(p)] = 1 replaced by j_𝔣(𝔭) = [𝔭O_K]: split 𝔭 = 𝔓𝔓ᶜ: T_𝔭² = [𝔓]² + [𝔓ᶜ]² + 2[𝔭O_K] = T_{𝔭²} + [𝔭O_K];
inert: T_𝔭 = 0, T_{𝔭²} = [𝔭O_K]; ramified 𝔭O_K = 𝔓²: T_𝔭² = [𝔓²] = T_{𝔭²}. If 𝔭 | 𝔣 no ideal of I_K(𝔣) has norm 𝔭 or 𝔭². □

**Lemma 4 (dual identity).** Let A ∈ Cl_F, 𝔯 := 𝔯_A, b supported on admissible 𝔫 ∈ A, X > 0. For (K,𝔣) ∈ 𝒪 and C ∈ G_{K,𝔣} put
S_C := Σ_{𝔞∈C, 𝔞⊆O_K} b(N_{K/F}𝔞). Then
- (i) Σ_{χ∈Ĝ_{K,𝔣}} |Σ_𝔫 b(𝔫)λ_χ(𝔫)|² = |G_{K,𝔣}| Σ_C |S_C|²;
- (ii) with R(m) := {u ∈ U/U² : mu ≫ 0} (fixed representatives) and, for μ ∈ F, an integral ideal 𝔩, X' > 0 and squarefree 𝔠,

  𝒩_𝔠(μ; 𝔩; X') := Σ_{(K,𝔣)∈𝒪} Φ(N(𝔣²𝔡_{K/F})/X') η_𝔣(𝔠) #{α ∈ 𝔩O_𝔣 : N_{K/F}α = μ},

  𝒬_A(b) := Σ_{(K,𝔣)∈𝒪} ω(K,𝔣)Φ(N(𝔣²𝔡_{K/F})/X) Σ_C|S_C|²
         = Σ_{𝔫,𝔫'} b(𝔫)b̄(𝔫') Σ_{𝔠𝔴 | (𝔫,𝔫'), (𝔠,𝔴)=1} μ(𝔴) Σ_{u∈R(ν_𝔫ν_𝔫')} 𝒩_𝔠(ν_𝔫ν_𝔫'u; 𝔴𝔠𝔯; X/N𝔴²);

- (iii) for μ ≫ 0 and 𝔩 with μO_F ⊆ 𝔩², writing α_t for a root of x² − tx + μ, K_t := F(α_t), 𝔣(t) := 𝔣(α_t;𝔩) (Lemma 1(d)):

  𝒩_𝔠(μ;𝔩;X') = 𝒩⁰ + 2 Σ_{t∈𝔩, t²−4μ ≪ 0} Σ_{𝔶 | 𝔣(t), (𝔣(t)/𝔶, S₀)=1} Φ(N(t²−4μ)/(N(𝔩𝔶)²X')) η_{𝔣(t)/𝔶}(𝔠)   (in the field K_t),

  where 𝒩⁰ := Σ_{(K,𝔣)∈𝒪} Φ(N(𝔣²𝔡)/X')η_𝔣(𝔠)#{a ∈ 𝔩 : a² = μ} vanishes unless μ ∈ F^{×2}, and then 0 ≤ |𝒩⁰| ≤ 2#{(K,𝔣) ∈ 𝒪 : N(𝔣²𝔡) ≤ 2X'} ≪ X'^{1+ε}.

*Proof.* (i) Parseval on G_{K,𝔣}. (ii) Fix (K,𝔣); T_𝔫 = 0 unless 𝔫 is prime to 𝔣, and all 𝔫 ∈ A prime to 𝔣 have j_𝔣(𝔫) = j := j_𝔣(A) (Lemma 2(b)).
With S := Σ_𝔫 b(𝔫)T_𝔫 = Σ_C S_C C and S^∨ := Σ_C S̄_C C^{−1}, Lemma 2(b) and 𝔞 ↦ 𝔞ᶜ give S^∨ = j^{−1}Σ_𝔫 b̄(𝔫)T_𝔫, so by Lemma 3

  Σ_C |S_C|² = [1](SS^∨) = Σ_{𝔫,𝔫'} b(𝔫)b̄(𝔫') Σ_{𝔠|𝔤} η_𝔣(𝔠) [j·j_𝔣(𝔠)^{−1}](T_{𝔐_𝔠}),   𝔐_𝔠 := 𝔫𝔫'𝔠^{−2}.

Only 𝔠 prime to 𝔣 contribute. Since 𝔫𝔯 = ν_𝔫O_F with ν_𝔫 ∈ O_F prime to 𝔣 (𝔯 is in S₀ or trivial, and 𝔣 is prime to S₀), j = [𝔯O_K]^{−1}. So [𝔟] = j·j_𝔣(𝔠)^{−1}
iff 𝔟𝔠𝔯O_K ∈ P_{K,O_F}(𝔣), and by Lemma 2(a) the integral 𝔟 ∈ I_K(𝔣) with N𝔟 = 𝔐_𝔠 and this class correspond bijectively to the O_𝔣^×-orbits of
𝒜 := {α ∈ 𝔠𝔯O_𝔣 : N_{K/F}(α)O_F = 𝔫𝔫'𝔯², αO_K prime to 𝔣} via α ↦ α(𝔠𝔯)^{−1}O_K (𝔠𝔯 is prime to 𝔣, so 𝔠𝔯O_K ∩ O_𝔣 = 𝔠𝔯O_𝔣 locally everywhere).
*Units.* Put m := ν_𝔫ν_𝔫'. Every α ∈ 𝒜 has Nα = mu₀ with u₀ ∈ U, mu₀ ≫ 0, and u₀ = uε² with u ∈ R(m) unique and ε ∈ U unique up to sign; so with
𝒜' := {α ∈ 𝒜 : Nα ∈ mR(m)}, each U-orbit of 𝒜 meets 𝒜' in exactly one pair ±α (if εα, α ∈ 𝒜' then ε² = 1). O_𝔣^× ⊇ U acts freely on 𝒜, and
[O_𝔣^× : U] = q(O_𝔣)|μ(O_𝔣)|/2 (the definitions in §0). Hence |𝒜/O_𝔣^×| = |𝒜'|/(2[O_𝔣^×:U]) = |𝒜'|/ω(K,𝔣), i.e.

  ω(K,𝔣)·[j·j_𝔣(𝔠)^{−1}](T_{𝔐_𝔠}) = Σ_{u∈R(m)} #{α ∈ 𝔠𝔯O_𝔣 : Nα = mu, αO_K prime to 𝔣}.

*Coprimality.* By Lemma 2(e) the last condition is (𝔫𝔫', 𝔣) = 1. If a prime of 𝔪𝔪' divides 𝔣 the count is 0 anyway (Lemma 2(c): v_𝔭(Nα) = 1), and
primes of 𝔠 do not divide 𝔣; so the condition may be replaced by (𝔤/𝔠, 𝔣) = 1 = Σ_{𝔴|𝔤/𝔠}μ(𝔴)1[𝔴 | 𝔣]. For 𝔴 | 𝔣, Lemma 2(c) (with v_𝔭(Nα) = 2 at
𝔭 | 𝔴) gives #{α ∈ 𝔠𝔯O_𝔣 : Nα = mu} = #{α ∈ 𝔴𝔠𝔯O_{𝔣/𝔴} : Nα = mu}; and N((𝔣/𝔴)²𝔡) = N(𝔣²𝔡)/N𝔴², η_𝔣(𝔠) = η_{𝔣/𝔴}(𝔠) ((𝔠,𝔴) = 1), and
𝔣 ↦ 𝔣/𝔴 is a bijection of {𝔣 prime to S₀ : 𝔴 | 𝔣} onto {𝔣 prime to S₀} (𝔴 | 𝔫 is prime to S₀). Summing over (K,𝔣) ∈ 𝒪 gives (ii).
(iii) The α ∈ 𝔩O_𝔣 ∩ F = 𝔩 give 𝒩⁰; |η| ≤ 1, at most two a, and |{(K,𝔣)}| ≪ X'^{1+ε} with N(𝔣²𝔡) ≤ 2X'. For α ∉ F with Nα = μ ≫ 0, t = Tr α
satisfies t² − 4μ ≪ 0, K ≅ K_t, and each t gives exactly two (K, α) (the two roots). By Lemma 1(d), α ∈ 𝔩O_𝔣 iff t ∈ 𝔩 and 𝔣 | 𝔣(t); writing

## 4. The family large sieve

**Theorem 1.** Let 0 < δ ≤ 1/100, X ≥ X₀(F,δ), 1/2 + 2δ ≤ σ ≤ 9/10, |t'| ≤ X^δ, 2 ≤ V ≤ X^{1/2}, N := 2^j with V/4 ≤ N ≤ X^{1−4δ}, and b = b_{j,t'} or

For comparison, the per-field sieve [CMQ] Lemma 3 summed over 𝒦(X) gives X^{1+o(1)}(N + X^{1/2})N^{1−2σ}; X^{3/2+o(1)} = Σ_K h_K is the family size.

### 4.1 Positivity, classes, and the dual sum
Write b = Σ_{A∈Cl_F}b_A, b_A := b·1_{[𝔫]=A}. For K ∈ 𝒦(X), Lemma 4(i) and Cauchy–Schwarz give Σ_χ|Σ_𝔫 bλ_χ|² = h_KΣ_C|S_C(b)|² ≤ h_Kh_FΣ_AΣ_C|S_C(b_A)|².

### 4.2 Lattice points of ideal lattices
**Lemma 7 (boxes).** Let 𝔞 be a fractional ideal of F and t₀ ∈ F⊗R. Every box Π_v[c_v − Z_v, c_v + Z_v] contains at most 2^{n−1}(1 + 2^{n+1}Π_vZ_v/N𝔞)

Σ_{x∈Λ'∖0}Π_v(1 + |x_v|)^{−A} ≤ C(n,A)R^{1−A}log^{n−1}(2R).

*Proof.* Nonzero k ∈ 𝔞 have |N(k)| = Π_v|k_v| ≥ N𝔞. Cut the box into m^n sub-boxes with m := ⌈(2^{n+1}ΠZ_v/N𝔞)^{1/n}⌉; two points of one sub-box differ by
k ∈ 𝔞 with Π|k_v| ≤ 2^nΠZ_v/m^n < N𝔞, so k = 0 (this is the box argument of [CMQ] Lemma 2). For the sum: nonzero x ∈ Λ' have Π|x_v| ≥ R; the set

applied to the ideal lattice scaled by w), and no nonzero point has Π(1+|x_v|) < R. Sum dyadically over Z ≥ R. □

### 4.6 The unit sum turns generator-dependent symbols into Hecke characters
Expanding A₂, A₀, h_μ(𝔢) (by (S)) and 1[μ ≫ 0], every inner sum above becomes a finite combination, with coefficients ≪ C_F^{ω(𝔢)}|ϑ|, of

  Σ_{u∈U/U²}Ξ(u) · Σ_{𝔫₂}β(𝔫₂)(N𝔫₂)^{z−1/2}Ψ⁰_{κ,T₁}(𝔫)Ξ(ν_𝔫) · Σ_{𝔫₂'}β̄(𝔫₂')(N𝔫₂')^{z−1/2}Ψ⁰_{κ',T₁}(𝔫')Ξ(ν_𝔫'),

ν_𝔫, ν_𝔫', u, by multiplicativity). Now Σ_{u∈U/U²}Ξ(u) = 2ⁿ·1[Ξ|_U = 1], so only Ξ trivial on U survive.

**Lemma 11.** Let Ξ = Ξ_{a,ψ,S,q} with q squarefree prime to 2S₀ and Ξ|_U = 1. Put 𝔪_Ξ := 8·(Π_{𝔭∈S}𝔭)·q·∞_a (∞_a: the real places with a_v = 1).

(b) Fix generators [𝔤_i] (1 ≤ i ≤ r) of Cl_F ≅ ⊕Z/h_i with primes 𝔤_i ∉ P_F, and γ_i with γ_iO_F = 𝔤_i^{h_i}; put Θ̃(𝔤_i) := ρ_i(Ξ(γ_i)) (ρ_i(1) := 1,

of I extending Θ, and χ_Ξ := Θ̃|_{I(𝔪_Ξ)} is a ray class character mod 𝔪_Ξ whose conductor has odd part prime to S₀ equal to q.
(c) For admissible 𝔫₂ = κ𝔪 with (𝔪, κT₁) = 1 (and 𝔫 = 𝔠𝔴𝔫₂ ∈ A): Ξ(ν_𝔫) = Θ̃(𝔠𝔴κ𝔯_A)·χ_Ξ(𝔪), a constant of modulus 1 times χ_Ξ(𝔪).

*Proof.* (a) Ξ(εx) = Ξ(x) for ε ∈ U. If x ≫_a 0 and x ≡ 1 mod^× 𝔪_Ξ then sgn^a(x) = 1, ψ(x) = 1 (1 + 8O_𝔭 ⊆ 1 + 4𝔭O_𝔭 ⊆ (O_𝔭^×)²), and [x/𝔭] = 1 for
𝔭 | Sq. (b) Ξ(g_𝔞) does not depend on the choice of g_𝔞 (Ξ|_U = 1). Multiplicativity: if e_i + e_i' = e_i'' + h_ic_i (c_i ∈ {0,1}) then g_{𝔞𝔞'} =

### 4.7 The secondary terms (Re z = δ/2): the classical large sieve suffices

**Lemma 12 (large sieve for ray class characters; elementary).** Let 𝔉 be a finite set of ray class characters of F (finite order) such that χχ̄' is
nonprincipal for χ ≠ χ' in 𝔉 and is a character modulo some 𝔪_{χχ'} with N𝔪_{χχ'} ≤ 𝔔. Then for L' ≥ 2 and γ supported on N𝔪 ≤ L':
Σ_{χ∈𝔉}|Σ_𝔪 γ_𝔪χ(𝔪)|² ≤ C(F,ε)(L' + |𝔉|𝔔^{1/2+ε})L'^εΣ|γ_𝔪|².

*Proof.* Split γ into O(log L') pieces supported on P < N𝔪 ≤ 2P (Cauchy–Schwarz costs log L'). For one piece, by duality it suffices to bound
Σ_{P<N𝔪≤2P}|Σ_χc_χχ(𝔪)|² ≤ Σ_𝔪 w(N𝔪/P)|Σ_χc_χχ(𝔪)|² with a fixed w ∈ C_c^∞((1/2,3)), w ≥ 1 on [1,2]. Expand: the diagonal is ≤ CPΣ|c_χ|².
For χ ≠ χ', Σ_𝔪 w(N𝔪/P)χχ̄'(𝔪) = (2πi)^{−1}∫_{(2)}L(s,χχ̄')w̃(s)P^sds (w̃ entire), moved to Re s = −ε: L(s,χχ̄') is entire,
|L(−ε+it, χχ̄')| ≤ C(𝔔(1+|t|)ⁿ)^{1/2+2ε} (functional equation and convexity for the primitive character; the Euler factors at the primes of 𝔪 not
in the conductor are ≤ 2^{ω(𝔪)} ≪ 𝔔^ε), and w̃ decays rapidly. So the off-diagonal is ≤ C𝔔^{1/2+3ε}Σ_{χ≠χ'}|c_χc_χ'| ≤ C𝔔^{1/2+3ε}|𝔉|Σ|c_χ|². □

Apply it, as in [R3] §3.6, for fixed (a, ψ, S, 𝔶₂, 𝔶₀, 𝔱, T₂, T₃, T₄, T₅, φ) to the sum over T₁ (it enters only through q = T₁T₅ and the condition
(𝔫₂, T₁) = 1, which we keep in χ_Ξ(𝔪) = 0 for (𝔪,T₁) ≠ 1). 𝔉 := {χ_{Ξ_{a,ψ,S,T₁T₅}}φ : N(T₁) ≤ E₀/N(T₂T₃T₄T₅²), Ξ|_U = 1}: for T₁ ≠ T₁' the quotient
ramifies at the primes of T₁ΔT₁' (Lemma 11(b)), so it is nonprincipal, and it is a character mod lcm(𝔪_Ξ, 𝔪_Ξ'), of norm ≤ C_F Q² with
Q := E₀/N(T₂T₃T₄T₅) ≥ Nq; |𝔉| ≤ CQ. Lemma 12 thus gives the classical bound (L' + Q^{2+ε}) with L' := L/Nκ (resp. L/Nκ'). Here, exactly as in [R3],
Q² ≤ E₀²/(NκNκ') and E₀² ≤ L when N𝔴 ≤ G₂ (E₀²/L = 4^{n+2}N·N𝔴/(N𝔠X) ≤ 1), so Q² ≤ min(L/Nκ, L/Nκ'): the modulus range shrinks with κ, and the
classical large sieve does what Heath-Brown's quadratic large sieve did in [R3]. On Re z = δ/2: |N(𝔶₂𝔶₀𝔢𝔱)^{−2z}| ≤ 1, |(4ⁿ/X')^z| ≤ 4ⁿX'^{−δ/2},
|N𝔫₂^{z−1/2}| ≤ CL^{−(1−δ)/2} on supp β ⊂ [L/64, L]; Cauchy–Schwarz over T₁ and Lemma 12:

  Σ_{T₁}|B_{κ,q}(z)B'_{κ',q}(z)| ≪ X^{ε}(L/Nκ)^{1/2}(L/Nκ')^{1/2}L^{−1+δ}A_κ^{1/2}A_{κ'}^{1/2} = X^εL^δ(NκNκ')^{−1/2}A_κ^{1/2}A_{κ'}^{1/2},  A_κ := Σ_{κ|𝔫₂}d(𝔫₂)|β(𝔫₂)|².

The remaining sums (over T₂, …, T₅ with |ϑ|(NκNκ')^{−1/2} ≤ 2^{ω(T₄)}N(T₂T₃)^{−1/2}, and over 𝔶₂, 𝔶₀, 𝔱, a, ψ, S, φ: ≪ X^ε choices) are [R3] §3.6
verbatim: |𝔖_{𝔠,𝔴}(z)| ≪ X^{6δ}Σ_{𝔫₂}d(𝔫₂)⁴|β(𝔫₂)|² on Re z = δ/2. With |ζ_F^{(2S₀)}(δ + 2iτ)| ≪ (1 + |τ|)^{n} and the decay of Ǩ_n, the secondary

### 4.8 The main terms (z = ½): the pole is killed

**Lemma 13 (= [R3] Lemma 9).** Let κ'' := 𝔠𝔴κ be admissible and χ a ray class character (finite order) mod 𝔪_χ, χ(𝔪) := 0 if

(the weight N(𝔶₂𝔶₀𝔢𝔱)^{−1} with 𝔢 = T₁⋯T₅², |ϑ| ≤ 2^{ω(T₄)}NT₄NT₅, Σ_{T₁T₅=q}NT₁^{−1}NT₅^{−2} = Nq^{−1}Σ_{T₅|q}NT₅^{−1}, 𝓛𝓛' ≤ (𝓛² + 𝓛'²)/2),

  |𝔐_{𝔠,𝔴}| ≪ X^{9δ}N^{1−2σ}V N(𝔠𝔴)^{−1}·max ∫Σ_{Nq≤E₀}Nq^{−1}|L(½+δ+i(t'+u), χ_{Ξ_q}φ)|²(1+|u|)^{−n−4}du ≪ X^{11δ+nδ}N^{1−2σ}V/N(𝔠𝔴),

by the mean value Lemma 14 (max over a, ψ, S, φ; q runs over squarefree q prime to 2S₀ with Ξ_q := Ξ_{a,ψ,S,q} trivial on U). Hence
Σ_{𝔠,𝔴}N𝔠^{−1}√X'|𝔐_{𝔠,𝔴}| ≪ X^{(11+n)δ}√X V N^{1−2σ}Σ N𝔠^{−2}N𝔴^{−2} ≪ X^{(12+n)δ}X‖b‖² (V ≤ X^{1/2}; (1+|t'+u|)ⁿ ≤ X^{nδ}(1+|u|)ⁿ).

**Lemma 14 (mean value; Jutila's bound over F).** Fix a ∈ {0,1}ⁿ, ψ ∈ Ĝ₂, S ⊆ S₀, φ ∈ Ĉl_F. For Q ≥ 2, u ∈ R:
Σ_{q} |L(½+δ+iu, χ_{Ξ_q}φ)|² ≤ C(F,δ,ε)(Q(1+|u|)ⁿ)^{1+ε}, over squarefree q prime to 2S₀ with Nq ≤ Q and Ξ_q|_U = 1.

*Proof.* (i) *Reduction to a quadratic form.* Let χ := χ_{Ξ_q}φ and χ* the primitive character inducing it. By Lemma 11 and the construction, the
idele class character of χ has fixed components at the places above 2 (from ψ), at S and at ∞ (from a), the ramified quadratic component at each 𝔭 | q,
and is unramified elsewhere (φ is unramified). So the conductor of χ* is q𝔣₀ with 𝔣₀ | 8Π_{𝔭∈S}𝔭 depending only on (a, ψ, S); split the q-sum by 𝔣₀
and by dyadic ranges Q/2 < Nq ≤ Q. χ and χ* agree on ideals prime to 2, and L(s,χ) = L(s,χ*)·Π(1 − χ*(𝔭)N𝔭^{−s}) over the 𝔭 | 2 where χ* is
unramified (≤ n factors, each ≤ 2). By the approximate functional equation [IK, Thm 5.3], with s := ½+δ+iu and Z_q := (d_FN(q𝔣₀)(1+|u|)ⁿ)^{1/2},
|L(s,χ*)| ≤ |Σ_𝔞χ*(𝔞)N𝔞^{−s}V₁(N𝔞/Z_q)| + |Σ_𝔞χ̄*(𝔞)N𝔞^{s−1}g_q V₂(N𝔞/Z_q)|, where (y∂_y)^kV_j(y) ≪_{k,B}(1+y)^{−B} and |g_q| ≪ Z_q^{−2δ} (gamma
ratio; the root number has modulus 1). For N𝔞 ≤ Z_q, N𝔞^{δ}Z_q^{−2δ} ≤ N𝔞^{−δ}, so in both sums the coefficients are ≪ N𝔞^{−1/2−δ}(1 + N𝔞/Z_q)^{−B+1}.
The dependence of the cutoff on q is removed by a Fourier expansion of t ↦ V_j(y e^t) on t = log(Z_Q/Z_q) ∈ [0, log 2] (rapidly decaying coefficients,
uniformly in y) — after which the coefficients c_𝔞 do not depend on q. Splitting 𝔞 = 𝔞₂𝔞' (𝔞₂ supported on primes above 2) and using Cauchy–Schwarz
with weights N𝔞₂^{−δ}, it suffices to show, for c_𝔞 ≪ N𝔞^{−1/2−δ}(1 + N𝔞/Z)^{−B} supported on ideals prime to 2 (Z ≍ Z_Q), that
𝒱 := Σ_q w(Nq/Q)|Σ_𝔞 c_𝔞χ_q(𝔞)|² ≤ C(Q(1+|u|)ⁿ)^{1+3ε}, χ_q := χ_{Ξ_q}φ; and since χ̄_q is a character of the same kind (Ξ_q real, φ̄ ∈ Ĉl_F), the dual sum is
covered too. By positivity we may enlarge the q-range to all q prime to 2S₀ (with [x/q] := Π[x/𝔭]^{k_𝔭}; Lemma 11(a),(b) hold verbatim) and use a smooth
w ≥ 1_{[1/2,1]} supported in [1/4, 2].

## 9. Where it stops; dead ends

- *A₀ → 1*: as in [R3] §6, Theorem 1 without the N³X^{−1} term (pairs with a common factor 𝔴 of norm > X/N) would give the density hypothesis on
  average and A₀ = 1. Not attempted here.
- *All CM fields over F* (not only δ₀-rough ones): blocked exactly as [CMQ] §7.1 (small genus conductors need a Q-aspect log-free density with
  Deuring–Heilbronn repulsion for Hecke characters of F). Theorems 1 and 2 hold for all CM K/F; only Theorem 3 needs roughness.
- *F varying* (e.g. all quartic CM fields): the constants depend on F through d_F, h_F, S₀, the unit lattice and the 2-adic densities, and through
  (HT), (TZ) in [CMQ]; uniformity not attempted.
- *Sectors / infinite-type Hecke characters of K, and non-CM quadratic extensions*: the dual count acquires angular weights (sectors) or a
  relative unit group of positive rank (K/F not CM); not attempted.
- *Dead end (NOGO)*: choosing generators ν_𝔫 in a fundamental domain for the units does not make the quadratic symbols (ν_𝔫/𝔮) Hecke characters in 𝔫
  (it needs a smoothed Fourier expansion over the unit torus); the sum over U/U², which is forced anyway by the unit weight ω, does it exactly.
- *Dead end (NOGO)*: fixing reference ideals 𝔯_A ∈ A^{−1} without excluding them from the order conductors (𝔣 ∤ 𝔯_A) breaks Lemma 2(b) (𝔯_AO_K is not in
  I_K(𝔣)); restricting the family of orders to conductors prime to the finite set S₀ is harmless by positivity.
