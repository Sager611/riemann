# CRUX — where Masullo's trace formula sees only the zeros on (or near) the critical line, and where the proof would have to pay for it

Notation: C_F idele classes, φ ∈ D(C_F) with φ(C_F^1) = 0, M̂(c) := M(φ)(c|·|^{−1/2}), Ω(C_F) the quasi-characters, I = [−α, α].

**1. The strip restriction is topological and costs nothing (KNOWN).** The spectral side is the trace of convolution by φ on H = W_I^{s,2}(C_F)/closure(|·|^{1/2}Θ), Θ the global θ-functions (Mellin transforms Z_S(f_S, c)·L^S(c)). Its dual is spanned by χ(g)|g|^{ρ−1/2}(log|g|)^m at zeros ρ of L whose evaluations are continuous on W_I^{s,2}: Re ρ − ½ ∈ I, m < N(ρ) inside, m < min{N, ⌊(s+1)/2⌋} on the edges (Thm 10.2.1.1 p. 440, (10.2.2.5), (10.3.2.3) p. 454). Off-strip zeros drop out because their evaluations are discontinuous (§1.4.8, p. 31: "GRH is a topological phenomenon"). This is Connes' L²_δ theorem (1999, Thm 1: critical zeros, cap = largest n < (1+δ)/2, equal to (10.2.1.6) for even s) and Meyer's strip realizations (2005); it holds for any θ-realizable Dirichlet series, Davenport–Heilbronn included, and proves nothing about zeros.

**2. The geometric kernel is defined as the complement.** Def. 10.4.3.7 (p. 473): k^geom := φ(xy^{−1}) − k^spec. Since φ(1) = 0, ∫k^geom(x,x) = −Σ_{Re c−½ ∈ int I} N(c) M̂(c) *by definition* ((12.5.1.4), p. 520). All content lies in computing the same diagonal integral geometrically.

**3. The written geometric pieces are local and blind to I and s.** k^geom is cut along divisorial strata of GL₁(A_F): k = k_S + Σ_{p∉S} k_p, k_{p_k} := (α_{k+1} − α_k)k^geom (11.3.1.4, a definition). Thm 11.3.2.1 (p. 495; a = ½, s > 1, α ≥ 0): each stratum trace is (log q_v/μ_v(O_v^×)) Σ_{k∈Z} φ(π_v^k), the v-part of the prime side of Weil's formula, 0 once q_v exceeds supp φ. Thm 12.4.1.1 (p. 514): the S-local remnant obeys Connes' S-local formula ([Co §VII Thm 4]). Neither involves I, s or zeros of L; both hold for every Euler product.

**4. The decisive identity implies GRH.** The proof of Thm 12.5.1.1 (p. 520) needs
  (★) ∫k(x,x) = ∫k_S(x,x) + Σ_{p∉S} ∫k_p(x,x)  and  (★★) ∫_{C_F} k_S(x,x) = Weil's local terms − M(φ)(|·|^{1/2}) − M(φ)(|·|^{−1/2}),
the latter identifying the global remnant on C_F with the S-local kernel k^PW_{S,φ,λ,λ′} on J_{F,S} of Thm 12.4.1.1 (which has no pole terms). The asserted right sides do not depend on I. For α > ½ the resulting identity is Weil's explicit formula (PROBES P1–P5: checked to 10⁻⁸ for Q, Q(i), Q(√−3), Q(√5), a cubic field). Subtracting Weil's formula, (★)+(★★) at width α give Σ_{|Re ρ−½|>α} N(ρ)M̂(ρ) + Σ_{edges}(N − cap)M̂ = 0 for all admissible φ, i.e. no zero with |Re ρ − ½| > α (and edge zeros within the cap); for arbitrarily small α this is GRH. So (★)+(★★) imply GRH (GRH alone would not prove them as kernel identities). (★) equates the trace of an infinite sum of kernels with the sum of the traces; it needs trace-norm (or equivalent) control of the strata expansion in W_I^{s,2}, which is exactly what off-strip zeros would destroy (Connes' "resonances"). The manuscript supports (★) by citing the definition (11.3.1.4) and (★★) by citing Thm 12.4.1.1; the section meant to prove both, §12.3 "Putting the pieces together: global to S-local deconstruction of the geometric kernel" (p. 514), is empty: footnote "Include section."; §11.3.1, the only description, is an overview that "omit[s] several items" (p. 494).

**5. The same steps, as written, prove a false statement (Sobolev test; PROBES P12).** Take α = 0 (allowed by Def. 10.4.3.7, Thms 10.4.2.8, 11.3.2.1; it is the setting of the restated Thm 13.3.1.1) and Sobolev degrees s = 4 and s = 6 (allowed: s ≥ 3 in Def. 10.4.3.7, s > 1 in Thm 11.3.2.1; Thm 12.4.1.1 has no s). By (10.3.2.3) (p. 454) ∫k(x,x) = −Σ_crit min{N, ⌊(s+1)/2⌋} M̂: caps 2 and 3. Let F = K₁₂, the Galois closure of x⁴ + 8x + 12 (group A₄), χ = 1. Artin: ζ_{K₁₂} = ζ_{k₃}·L(ρ₃)³; the Hardy function of L(ρ₃) changes sign on (1.80, 1.83) (P6c), so ζ_{K₁₂} has a zero on Re s = ½ of multiplicity ≥ 3, and ∫k(x,x) differs between s = 4 and s = 6 for suitable φ. The asserted right sides of (★)+(★★) are the same for s = 4 and 6. For p ∉ S the strata traces vanish for every cap (all places above p have one residue degree f and χ = 1, so the poles of Π_{v|p} L_v form one progression 2πiZ/(f log p) of multiplicity g, and Poisson gives (f log p)Σ_m φ(p^{fm}) = 0), so Thm 11.3.2.1 is not the culprit there. Hence (★) or (★★) is false for some admissible s: the gluing as written proves too much. Comparing with Weil instead gives the same conclusion without the on-line zero: at s = 4 the chain would force Σ (N − min{N,2}) M̂ + Σ_{off-line} N M̂ = 0, impossible since the zeros of L(ρ₃) contribute positive weight. The manuscript's only stated reason for s ≥ 2[F:Q] (Remark 11.2.2.2, p. 491; p. 533: local pole multiplicities in the p-strata) is idle here. The threshold is global, not local: on K₁₂ the a-line chain is consistent exactly when the cap ⌊(s+1)/2⌋ is at least the largest multiplicity of a critical zero of ζ_{K₁₂} (≥ 3, from L(ρ₃)³), i.e. s ≥ 5, whereas the manuscript's threshold s ≥ 2[F:Q] = 24 comes from local pole multiplicities; at the author's own s = 2[F:Q] the a-line gluing holds iff every non-central critical zero has N ≤ [F:Q] — the multiplicity bound it is then used to prove. Any correct version of §12.3 must depend on s and on α, i.e. use global information on zeros of L in |Re s − ½| > α; nothing written supplies it (the only global inputs are non-vanishing and growth of 1/L on Re s = 1, Titchmarsh ordinates, convexity). Davenport–Heilbronn: steps 1–2 apply verbatim to any θ-realizable Dirichlet series (true and empty); step 3 needs only local Euler factors; the one step that could separate L from a Davenport–Heilbronn function is the missing one.

**6. Status.** Steps 1–3 KNOWN in substance (Connes, Meyer, Poisson summation per place); step 4 NEW, unproved, implies GRH, and as written (independent of s and α) false at α = 0 for some s ∈ {4, 6} on K₁₂ (independent reviewer: D1 CORRECT); the deduction of GRH from (12.5.1.1) (§1.4.5; Ch. 13 empty) is standard once (12.5.1.1) holds for all α > 0 (Connes 1999 already shows trace formula ⇒ RH).
