## Transfers, tests, verdicts
**T1 (cleanest; N13, N7): the passive ξ-network.** Under RH Z(w) = ξ′/ξ(1/2 + w) is Foster's lossless form (LC tanks, C = 1/2, L_n = 2/γ_n²); in general Z is positive real on Re w > 0 ⟺ RH (Lagarias) ⟺ E_h(z) = ξ(1/2 + h − iz) is Hermite–Biehler for all h > 0. Unconditionally the network shifted by h > T_* − 1/2 = 0.37338 (Lean-checked half-plane) is passive; the shift is exactly the zero-free half-plane. Built from Euler factors (open lossless lines of delay (log p)/2), ξ′/ξ = θ(P)/2 − Z_P(s) + (archimedean) + (pole) + (primes > P): the lossless Euler network enters with the wrong sign and is balanced by a divergent resistor θ(P)/2, which closes only on Re s > 1 (derivations D1).
- Test ([t1_pr.py](code/t1_pr.py), [t1_pr.jsonl](results/t1_pr.jsonl); t ∈ [1, 200], step 0.1): G(ε, t) = Re F′/F(1/2 + ε + it). ξ: G > 0 on ε = 0.02, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3 (minimum 2εΣγ^{−2} at t = 1). mv: G < 0 only on [39.2, 39.4] for ε < 0.15. dh: G < 0 only at the heights of the zeros with β − 1/2 > ε: ε = 0.02 at 85.7, 114.2, 166.5, 176.7; ε = 0.1 at 85.7, 114.2, 176.7; ε = 0.2 at 85.7, 176.7; ε = 0.3 only on [85.65, 85.75] (minimum −114.8).
- Adversarial (a): the passivity criterion fails for dh and mv exactly where their zeros are; no mechanism proves passivity without the zeros, and the Euler-product decomposition is not passive term by term. (b) It is Hermite–Biehler/de Branges (Lagarias, Sondow–Dumitrescu); on one line it is zero detection at the zero's own height. Verdict: restatement.

**T2 (best testable; N6, N1, N13): the Cauer RC ladder of the zero network from Taylor data only.** Z(p) = Σ_ρ 1/(w_ρ + p) = Σ_k (−1)^k σ_{k+1}p^k with σ_k = Σ w_ρ^{−k} from the Taylor coefficients of F at s = 1/2 (moments of F's θ-kernel; no zeros used). Cauer's second form is the S-fraction c₁/(1 + c₂p/(1 + c₃p/…)); all c_k > 0 ⟺ Stieltjes moments ⟺ (Grommer) all w_ρ > 0 ⟺ RH for F. With m_k = σ_{k+1} and P(x) = Σ a_i x^i, the Hankel form Σ a_ia_j m_{i+j} = Σ_ρ g(z_ρ)², g(z) = z^{−1}P(z^{−2}), is Weil-type positivity on a rational test family: the Gram-matrix certificate method with a low-height basis.
- Test ([t2_cauer.py](code/t2_cauer.py); trapezoid moments at 250–850 digits; σ₁(ξ) = 0.02310499311541897078893 reproduced): ξ: c₁ … c₂₂₀ > 0. dh: negative exactly c₁₄₉, c₁₅₁, c₂₁₈, c₂₂₀, all other c₁ … c₂₃₂ positive (dps 650 and 850 with two step sizes agree to 3 decimals in log₁₀ c_k; dps 450 drifts from c₁₈₅ on). DH has 43 and 61 on-line zeros below 85.70 and 114.16 ([t2_count.py](code/t2_count.py)), so the two off-line pairs are atoms 44–45 and 64–65 and are flagged at Hankel orders 75 and 109 (1.7× their rank). mv: first negative c₂₆, c₂₈, c₂₉, c₃₂, c₃₆, c₃₉ for δ = 0.3, 0.15, 0.05, 0.01, 10⁻³, 10⁻⁴: about +3.5 indices per decade, unbounded as δ → 0.
- Adversarial (a): the ladder does flag dh and mv, but only at order ≈ 1.7 × rank with about 5 digits per order, and positivity of c₁ … c_K excludes no off-line pair at any height (a small enough δ passes): it certifies nothing finite, weaker than zero verification (height 3·10¹² would need order ~2·10¹³ and ~10¹⁴ digits). (b) It is Grommer/Polson Hankel positivity (R296, R307) and a Gram form. Verdict: restatement; detection orders measured.

**T3 (N5, N7): Kramers–Kronig / Bode.** KK holds for any causal stable response; the Bode gain–phase law holds iff the response is minimum phase (outer). For F(1/2 + a + w): minimum phase ⟺ no zero with β > 1/2 + a; at a = 0 this is Beurling's outer criterion, and Bode's sensitivity integral is Balazard–Saias–Yor's identity. Test ([t3_bode.py](code/t3_bode.py)): an off-line pair adds (2β − 1)/|ρ|² to the KK defect: DH 8.40·10⁻⁵, 2.31·10⁻⁵, 5.37·10⁻⁶, 1.44·10⁻⁵ (total 1.27·10⁻⁴; explore13's floor 2.54·10⁻⁴ counts the 8 zeros of the quartets), mv 1.95·10⁻⁴, a pair at height 3·10¹² with β − 1/2 = 10⁻³: 2.2·10⁻²⁸. Adversarial (a): DH and mv fail it, with defects far below any line-data accuracy at large height; (b) restates Beurling/BSY (explore13). Verdict: restatement.

**T4 (N2): diffusion, Warburg and kinetics.** The finite Warburg response is θ₃, its two classical series are Jacobi's transformation, and the Mellin transform of its relaxation kernel is 2π^{−2w}Γ(w)ζ(2w); Biane–Pitman–Yor's law (scaled Brownian-bridge range, Mellin transform 2ξ) has a square with Laplace transform (√(2λ)/sinh√(2λ))², the squared membrane-permeation transfer function. Marcus–Hush–Chidsey is a Gaussian smoothing of the Fermi function (the de Bruijn–Newman direction t < 0, where Λ ≥ 0 creates non-real zeros); Fermi–Dirac integrals give Γ(s)η(s); Butler–Volmer zeros lie on Re η = 0 for every transfer coefficient. Adversarial (a): DH has the same structure with a positive kernel: Ψ(v) = 2e^{3v/2}Σ a_n n e^{−πn²e^{2v}/5} is even (functional equation) and Ψ > 0 on ℝ (θ(x) ≥ 0.67e^{−πx/5} for x ≥ 1; [chk_dh.py](code/chk_dh.py): Λ(1/2) = 1.43319216877500844 reproduced), so Λ_DH(1/2 + iz)/Λ_DH(1/2) is the characteristic function of a probability density and still has off-line zeros; a periodic diffusion cell (flat torus ℝ²/(ℤ ⊕ 5^{−1/2}ℤ)) has spectral zeta (4π²)^{−s} × Epstein zeta of x² + 5y² (class number 2), with infinitely many zeros in σ > 1 (Davenport–Heilbronn 1936). (b) Riemann's second proof, BPY (R10.2, R342). Verdict: DH-blind.

**T5 (N5, N1): positive relaxation spectra (DRT positivity) on Nyman–Beurling.** DRT inversion imposes G ≥ 0. In the Báez-Duarte problem min ‖χ − Σ_{k≤N} c_k{1/(kt)}‖², c₁ ≥ 0 alone forces d² ≥ (1 − ln²2)/2 = 0.259773 for every N (on t > 1 the error is (Σc_k/k)/t, on (1/2, 1) it is 1 + c₁ − (Σc_k/k)/t; derivations D5), while the natural coefficients are c_k = −μ(k). Test ([t5_posnb.py](code/t5_posnb.py), Vasyunin Gram, box-constrained coordinate descent): ζ with c_k ≥ 0: d⁺² = 0.2947, 0.2733, 0.27105, 0.271034 at N = 5, 10, 20, ≥ 40 (support 3–9, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23, 31); with c₁ free and c_k ≥ 0 for k ≥ 2: plateau 0.027225 from N = 40; with the Möbius sign pattern imposed (c_k·(−μ(k)) ≥ 0): 0.009659 at N = 160, against free d² = 0.009346. DH with c_k ≥ 0: plateau 0.06687 from N = 20 (free 0.0347 at N = 80). Adversarial (a): positivity stalls the RH-true and the RH-false function alike, so it measures sign structure, not zeros; (b) the sign pattern that works is Möbius's, i.e. Weil's signed prime kernel again. Verdict: dead.
