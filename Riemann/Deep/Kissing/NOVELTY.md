## The lattice M (KNOWN)
10. PARI/GP 2.19 (qfisom/qfauto, about 20 s): M is isometric to catalogue entry L_24.2 = ^((5+√13)/2)L_24,2, the cyclo-quaternionic extremal 3-modular lattice (G. Nebe, Some cyclo-quaternionic lattices, J. Algebra 199 (1998) 472–498; https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/~Gabriele.Nebe/LATTICES/L_24.2.html, entry dated 18 Jul 2014: det 3¹², min 6, kissing number 26208, Aut = SL₂(13)∘SL₂(3)). We found an explicit integral isometry. M ≅ √3·M* (3-modular). |Aut M| = 26208 = 2⁵·3²·7·13. M has 530712 vectors of norm 8, matching the extremal theta series. Nebe's table https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/~Gabriele.Nebe/LATTICES/ExtLat.html lists "≥ 1" extremal 3-modular lattice in dim 24.
11. So the 13104 lines are the minimal vectors of a known lattice, i.e. a known 26208-point kissing configuration in R²⁴. Its index-3⁶ embedding in Λ₂₄ is not stated in the sources read. Nebe's arXiv 1910.06587 says no extremal 3-modular lattice in dim 24 is the trace lattice of a Hermitian unimodular Z[ζ₃]-lattice; this matches θM* ≠ M. Aut(M) does not preserve our Leech overlattice (both qfauto generators fail), so the embedding is not canonical. Treat it as folklore, not as a new claim.
12. No source uses L_24.2, or any lattice, as an equator for a kissing configuration.
    To reproduce in gp: B = mathnf of {4e_i ± 4e_j, 2·(Golay rows), (−3,1²³)}; Mc = mathnf(concat(matkermod(F·B,3), 3·I)); GM = (B·Mc)ᵀ(B·Mc)/8. Then qfisom(GM, Gram of L_24.2) ≠ 0, qfisom(GM, 3·GM⁻¹) ≠ 0 and qfauto(GM)[1] = 26208.
