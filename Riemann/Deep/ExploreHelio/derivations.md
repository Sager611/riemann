# ExploreHelio derivations (explore23)

Notation: zeros ρ = 1/2 + iγ_ρ with γ_ρ = −i(ρ − 1/2) (complex for off-line zeros); N̄(t) = θ(t)/π + 1 for ζ; L̄ = Σ w(γ_n) log(γ_n/2π) / Σ w(γ_n) (weighted mean log-density). Window w(t) = sin²(π(t − T1)/(T2 − T1)) on [T1, T2].

## D1. Duvall/Abel inversions
**(a) One sequence, radial modes (1-D cavity with acoustic cutoff).** WKB with a hard wall at τ = 0 and cutoff profile ω_c(τ): (1/π)∫_0^{τ_t} √(ω² − ω_c(τ)²) dτ = m(ω), m = N̄(ω) − N̄(2π), N̄(ω) = (ω/2π) ln(ω/2πe). With E = ω², V = ω_c², dA/dE = (1/2)∫(E − V)^{−1/2} dτ, Abel inversion gives τ(V) = 2∫_{V0}^{V} m′(E)(V − E)^{−1/2} dE with m′(E) = ln(√E/2π)/(4π√E), V0 = (2π)². Substituting E = V sin²φ:
τ(ω_c) = (1/π)[(π/2 − φ0) ln(ω_c/2π) + ∫_{φ0}^{π/2} ln sin φ dφ], sin φ0 = 2π/ω_c; ω_c(τ) = 4π e^{2τ}(1 + o(1)).
Checked: forward WKB reproduces N̄(ω) − N̄(2π) to 2·10⁻⁴ relative at ω = 10 … 200 and 4·10⁻⁵ at ω = 1000, 5000 (grid-limited); τ monotone; ω_c/(4πe^{2τ}) = 1.000025 at ω_c = 10⁶ (results/duvall.json). This is Wu–Sprung's potential (1993) with x ↔ τ, V ↔ ω_c² (their E = γ, ours E = γ²).
**(b) The L-function family.** N(T, χ) = (T/2π) ln(qT/2πe) − χ(−1)/8 + S(T, χ) + O(1/T) (+1 for ζ, pole). Hence π(n + α)/γ_n = F(qγ_n) + πS/γ_n with F(w) = (1/2) ln(w/2πe): Duvall's law with w = q·ω (degree L ↔ 1/q); the constant count phase −χ(−1)/8 (+1 for ζ's pole; it comes from Im log Γ((1/2 + a + it)/2) ≈ … + (2a − 1)π/8) plays the role of Duvall's surface phase α. Duvall: F(w) = ∫_{r_t}^{R} (a⁻² − w⁻²)^{1/2} dr/r, a = c/r. With ζ = ln(R/r), X = a⁻², Y = w⁻², Abel inversion: ζ(X) = (2/π)∫_X^{X_s} (−dF/dY)(Y − X)^{−1/2} dY. For F = −(1/4) ln Y + const: ζ(X) = (1/(π√X)) arctan √((X_s − X)/X) → 1/(2√X) = a/2 as X_s → ∞, i.e. **c(r) = 2r ln(R/r)**. Forward check with a surface cut a_s: F(w) = (1/2) ln(2w/(e a_s)) (numerically 2.14917 vs 2.14916 at w = 1, 4.45185 vs 4.45174 at w = 100). Matching gives q = 4π/(a_s L).

## D2. Glitches = explicit formula
**Direct (Landau).** For h(t) = w(t)e^{iλt}, the explicit formula gives Σ_γ h(γ) = smooth − (1/2π)Σ_n Λ(n) n^{−1/2}[ŵ(log n − λ) + ŵ(−log n − λ)], ŵ(u) = ∫w(t)e^{iut}dt. At λ = log n the first term is ŵ(0) = ∫w; dividing by Σw(γ_n) ≈ (1/2π)∫w log(t/2π) dt:
G(log n) = Σ_n w(γ_n) n^{iγ_n} / Σ w(γ_n) = −Λ(n)/(√n L̄) + O(leakage). In stellar terms: a step in impedance at acoustic depth ½ log n with reflection ∝ Λ(n)/√n (S(t) ∋ −(1/π)Λ(n)/(√n log n) sin(t log n)).
**Dual (Gonek 1984, Lemma 5).** On ρ = 1/2 + iγ, χ(1 − ρ) = e^{2iθ(γ)}. Σ_{γ≤T} χ(1 − ρ) m^{−ρ} = (1/2πi)∮ χ(1−s) m^{−s} ζ′/ζ(s) ds; the right edge is −Σ_n Λ(n)(1/2πi)∫χ(1 − s)(mn)^{−s} ds = −Σ_{n ≤ T/2πm} Λ(n) e(−mn) + E = −ψ(T/2πm) + E. So Σ_γ e^{2iθ(γ)} m^{−iγ} ≈ −√m ψ(T/2πm) ≈ −T/(2π√m), per zero C_m ≈ −1/(√m L̄) for EVERY m ≥ 1 (frequency d/dγ(2θ − γ log m) = log(γ/2πm): a glitch at depth ½ log m below the 'surface' ½ log(T/2π)). A sampling alias of the direct glitches would instead give −Λ(m)/(√m L̄), zero at m = 6, 10, 12, …; measured C_6 = −0.05514 vs −1/(√6 L̄) = −0.05493 (results/glitch_zeta.json).

## D3. Off-line pairs in ordinate statistics
An off-line pair (γ̄, δ) has complex γ = γ̄ ∓ iδ. For any statistic Σ_ρ h(γ_ρ) with h(t) = w(t)e^{iλt}:
complex − ordinates(double) = Σ_pairs e^{iλγ̄} w(γ̄)[e^{λδ} + e^{−λδ} − 2] = 2(cosh λδ − 1) e^{iλγ̄} w(γ̄) (+ O(δ w′)),
complex − on-line only = 2 cosh(λδ) e^{iλγ̄} w(γ̄). The explicit formula holds for the complex list; so with the prime side the ordinate list shows a second-order residual −2(cosh λδ − 1)…/Σw and the sign-change list a zeroth-order one (Turing/Booker). Any statistic of the ordinates alone (gaps, second differences δ²γ_n, échelle ridges) is identical for an off-line pair (γ̄, δ), for an on-line double zero at γ̄ and, to O(gap), for a Lehmer pair: it cannot see δ.

## D4. Curvature charge in Weil's form
For real f on W = [−L/2, L/2], Q(f) = Σ_ρ F(γ_ρ)F(−γ_ρ), F(z) = ∫_W f e^{izx}dx; A = F·F*, F*(z) = conj F(conj z), is ≥ 0 on R (a SOLA/OLA averaging kernel of exponential type L). An off-line quadruple contributes 4 Re A(γ̄ − iδ) = 4A(γ̄) − 2δ²A″(γ̄) + O(δ⁴A⁗) (Re A(x − iδ) = Σ_k (−1)^k δ^{2k} A^{(2k)}(x)/(2k)!). So the pair = an on-line double zero plus a curvature charge −2δ²A″(γ̄): positive excess where A is concave (a SOLA kernel centred on γ̄), deficit where A is convex (tails, dips, nodes). Bernstein: |A″| ≤ L² sup A, so the charge is ≤ 2δ²L² sup A while every real zero adds A(γ_k) ≥ 0 — making Q negative is the window/null-steering problem of explore19, untouched by localisation. Checked (results/sola.json): charge = −2δ²A″ to < 1 % when δL ≤ 0.3, ≤ 2.3 % at δ = 0.15 and ≤ 9 % at δ = 0.3 (the δ⁴ term), L = 1 … 4.

## D5. Davenport–Heilbronn as two uncoupled cavities
f = c1 L(s, χ) + c̄1 L(s, χ̄), χ mod 5, χ(2) = i, c1 = (1 − iκ)/2. With Z(t) = ε^{1/2} Λ(1/2 + it, χ̄) (real for all real t), Λ(1/2 + it, χ) = ε^{1/2} Z(−t), so Λ_DH(1/2 + it) = u(Z(t) + Z(−t)), u = c1ε^{1/2}; reality of Λ_DH on the line forces u ∈ R (this fixes κ). Numerically Zdh/(Z(t) + Z(−t)) = 0.51978 with relative spread 6·10⁻¹¹ (results/mixed.json). The zeros of Z on t > 0 are those of L(s, χ̄), on t < 0 those of L(s, χ): DH on the line is the sum of the two Hardy functions, i.e. two cavities with no coupling term (no avoided crossings). Hermite–Kakeya–Obreschkoff interlacing is not the mechanism: up to 420 the merged zero sets (322 + 321) have 153 non-interlacing runs but DH has 9 off-line pairs, and 5 of the 9 off-line ordinates lie outside every run.
