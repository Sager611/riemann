# R006 — P5 barrier and the remaining nodes (own work, 2026-11-28)

## P5: certified near-criticality of Tolmachev's outer bodies
- Certificate (Borsuk/certs/p5_jung_0985.txt, check: python3 Borsuk/code/p5_cert.py Borsuk/certs/p5_jung_0985.txt 9849 10000, needs kissat and drat-trim built from github.com/arminbiere/kissat, github.com/marijnheule/drat-trim into Borsuk/.scratch/tools): 1192 exact rational points inside H(u*), u* = (1, 0, 0, 0) (ρ = 2/5; contact triangle = three vertices of the regular unit simplex inscribed in the Jung sphere, at height √(1/15)); membership slack ≥ 7.43·10⁻⁷ (60-digit arithmetic for the irrational centres, exact for |y|² ≤ 2/5 and y₄ ≤ 1/2); exact squared distances; 156,899 pairs at distance ≥ 0.9849; kissat UNSAT, drat-trim VERIFIED. So every 5-part partition of H(u*) has a part of diameter ≥ 0.9849. Upper side: the far graph at 0.995 on 40,000 boundary samples of H(u*) is 5-colourable, so the room left is between 0.5% and 1.5%.
- Tabu search (weaker evidence): no 5-colouring at threshold 0.97 at u = (0.7, 0, 1, 0), (0.6, 1, 0, 0), (0.3, 0, 0, 0) (1000–2800-point samples; not certified).
- Threshold 1: 5-colourings of the far graph on 20,000–40,000 boundary samples found at every tested u (77 parameter points: corners and edges of the u-cube, u₂ = 0 grid). So H(u) may be 5-partitionable, but with at most 1.5% slack at u*. (A tabu failure at threshold 1 at u = (0.75, 0, 0, 1) was refuted by kissat: SAT on the 4353-point reduced graph.)
- Convex 5-cell partitions (power diagrams, the form every box certificate needs): best found 1.007 (u = (0.6, 1, 0, 0), smooth-colouring + LP fit) and 1.070 (u*, evolution strategy); the 5 cones aligned with the simplex give 1.110 at u*.
- Margin needs more contacts: with all five Jung contacts the aligned cones give 0.874 (R004), but normal forms with 4 or 5 contacts have 6–10 parameters, against 4 parameters and 1.2·10⁴ certified boxes (75 min) for the 6-part proof.
- Verdict: dead. The 4-parameter normal form leaves ≤ 1.5% room for 5 parts at its Jung corner and no convex 5-cell partition below 1 is known anywhere in the hard region; the refinements that restore room multiply the parameter dimension beyond any certified box computation available here.
- P3 (finite reduction + exact computation) is the same method: same barrier.

## Other nodes
- P1 (universal cover cut into 5): known 4D covers: cube [0,1]⁴ (16 vertices pairwise ≥ 1: 16 parts), Jung's ball (r = 2/5 > 0.33: ≥ 6 parts by the spherical isodiametric bound of R003), Langi's ≤ 14-facet centrally symmetric polytopes (no partition known; Makeev: none above 20 facets). Finding a new cover is an unparametrised search; the normal-form method is its adaptive refinement (one outer body per contact datum) and is near-critical. (H(u) is not a set of diameter 1, so its near-criticality does not transfer to covers by implication.) Dead.
- P2 (constant width): the proved classes (smooth, centrally symmetric, revolution, belt) all have a single-valued or symmetric diametral map on most of the boundary; a general body has set-valued diametral relations at singular points (normal cones of angular radius up to arctan(√(3/8)/√(5/8)) = 37.8° at Reuleaux-simplex vertices), and no reduction of the general case to these classes exists; the remaining structure (circumradius, contacts) is P5. Bodies containing a regular unit simplex split into 5 (R004). Dead.
- P4 (topology): for a disproof, Z₂-index, genus and Lyusternik–Schnirelmann bounds on diametral pairs are capped at 5 by the direction map to S³ (R001 L5, also for compact sets at ε < 1 − 2/√5); for a proof, topological partitions need a fixed-point-free single-valued diametral map (smooth bodies, P2). Dead.
- D4b (spherical, circumradius r ≤ D/√2): r < 0.5477D is settled by the 5 simplex cones (parts ≤ 1.826r < D); for r ∈ [0.5477, 0.7071]D every point of X is a contact, so X is a special case of P5's normal forms (and the full sphere needs 6 parts once (r/D)² > 0.33, so a partition must adapt to X); structured spherical sets are D1. No further route. Dead.
- D5 (algebraic): every known algebraic construction is a two-distance set (Bondarenko, Jenrich–Brouwer: dead in ℝ⁴ by D3, ≤ 10 points), a tensor/projector square (Kahn–Kalai, OpenAI: dimensions k(k+1)/2 − 1 ∈ {2, 5, 9}, D6), or a cube/lattice subset (D2). Dead.
- D7 (small counterexamples): every counterexample has ≥ 11 points (R001 L4); the 11-vertex 6-critical Mycielskian M(K₅) is not realizable (each uᵢ is forced to the reflection of vᵢ in the opposite facet, at distance 2√(5/8) = 1.58 > 1). Beyond 11 vertices the 6-critical graphs are not enumerated and each candidate needs a real-algebraic realizability test; the structured finite families are D1, D2, D3. Dead (no finite search can close it).
- D9 (optimisation of β₅/diam over finite sets): best value in ℝ⁴ by multistart global optimisation 0.913149 (61 points; ball 0.912871), counterexample needs 1; the authors report their algorithms cannot reach Borsuk counterexamples (R004). Dead.
