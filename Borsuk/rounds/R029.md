# R029 — P13: illumination by 5 directions (own computation + literature, 2026-11-28)

- If 5 directions illuminate a body W (every boundary point x has a direction w with ⟨n, w⟩ < 0 for all normals n at x), W is covered by 5 smaller homothets (Boltyanski–Hadwiger), hence splits into 5 parts of smaller diameter. With a rotated regular simplex of directions Qd_j this is a per-point condition: every normal cone N(x) (radius r_x) must lie in an open hemisphere around some Qd_j; points with r_x < 14.48° always pass (the simplex directions have covering radius 75.52°).
- Numerics (Borsuk/code/p13_illum.py; normal cones of conv(F) from 60,000 sampled directions): the completion of two orthogonal unit triangles (cone radii up to 35.5°), the 4D rotated Reuleaux triangle (up to 42.7°) and a type-5 completion (up to 30.3°) are all illuminated by a rotated regular simplex of directions.
- Literature (scout, verified: arXiv:2304.10418, arXiv:2011.06398): for constant-width bodies the illumination number is open already in ℝ³ (conjecture 4, proved bound 6, Lassak) and in ℝ⁴ only ≤ 12 is known (Bezdek–Kiss); in high dimensions it grows exponentially. Illumination by d + 1 directions is therefore strictly harder than Borsuk in ℝ³ (proved) — not a shortcut; recorded as P13 (open, low rank).
