# R007 — P6: margins of the all-contact outer bodies; the "no unit pair" relaxation (own computation, 2026-11-28)

Node: P6/P8. Claim tested: with all contacts the outer bodies have room for 5 parts. Verdict: room is 3–6% near ρ = 0.30–0.33 for every contact type; tight but nonzero; the relaxation below and aligned simplex cones are the next tools.

## Survey (Borsuk/code/p6_survey.py; 8,000 boundary samples; tabu CEGAR at thresholds 0.90/0.94/0.97/1.0)
- Ω = B(0, √ρ) ∩ ⋂ B(pᵢ, 1) ∩ frame cuts, random admissible minimal contact sets: type 3 (triangle through 0, frame |x·v₁| ≤ 1/2, x·v₂ ≤ 1/2), type 4 (tetrahedron in x₄ = 0, x₄ ≤ 1/2), type 5 (4-simplex).
- 19 configurations (ρ = 0.300–0.331; rejection sampling favours small ρ): 18 have β₅(sample) ∈ (0.94, 0.97], one (type 3, ρ = 0.318) in (0.97, 1.0]. Denser samples raise β₅, so true room ≈ 1–5%.
- Convex 5-cell power diagrams for H(0.6, 1, 0, 0) by a softmax surrogate (p6_pd.py): 1.028 at best (LP fit of a smooth colouring: 1.007).

## Relaxation (valid, unused by the 6-part proofs in the middle range)
- A part may contain two points at distance > 1: they never lie in one set of diameter 1. The exact condition on a closed part P of the outer body: no pair of P at distance exactly 1. For P a finite union of convex cells this means: every cell has diameter < 1, and two cells of P are either close (all cross distances < 1) or far (all > 1), close being transitive inside P.
- Test on the 1192 certificate points of H(1,0,0,0): far graph at [0.985, ∞), [0.985, 1.10], [0.985, 1.05] UNSAT; band [0.985, 1.02] SAT. The obstruction needs pairs up to 1.05, so the relaxation helps only through cluster gaps; blob SAT (p6_blobs.py) with 300 blobs is too coarse (blob diameter 0.38, no far pairs); fine blobs in 4D give ~10⁹ clauses.

## Critical pairs of the simplex partition at ρ = 3/10
- The 5 simplex cones split B(0, √ρ) into parts of squared diameter (10/3)ρ: exactly 1 at ρ = 3/10, attained only by the 15 pairs (w_ab, w_cd), w_ab = −(v_a + v_b)/|v_a + v_b|, {a, b} ∩ {c, d} = ∅: the 10 directions w_ab with these pairs form the Petersen graph (adjacent w's at 80.4°, critical pairs at 131.8°).
- For ρ slightly above 3/10, a rotated simplex partition works on Ω if the excluded caps around −p_k (angular radius 180° − α ≈ 48°) remove a vertex cover of the Petersen graph (≥ 6 of the 10 directions; one cap can remove two adjacent w's, 80.4° apart). Next: test this alignment for 3, 4 and 5 contacts.

## Rotated simplex cones (Borsuk/code/p6_cones.py; apex z and rotation Q optimised by an evolution strategy on 3,000 boundary samples, checked on 30,000)
- type 3, ρ = 0.3138: 0.9807 (3k) / 1.0134 (30k); type 4, ρ = 0.3097: 0.9712 (3k) / 1.0026 (30k); type 5, ρ = 0.3007: 0.9553 (3k) / 0.982 (30k).
- Cones are borderline at ρ ≈ 0.31 (types 3, 4 above 1 on dense samples). Convex 5-cell partitions are too rigid there; the relaxation (far clusters) is the remaining freedom, and dense SAT discretisations of it are too large (pairs and close–close–far triples on ≥ 10⁵ cells).
- Open question that decides the middle range: the largest density of a closed subset of S³ avoiding the single angle α = 2 arcsin(1/(2√ρ)) (ρ ∈ [0.30, 0.35], α ∈ [115°, 132°]); if it is < 1/5 on the part of the sphere left in Ω, no partition of Ω into 5 parts without unit pairs exists.
