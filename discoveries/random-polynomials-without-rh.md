# Irreducibility of random polynomials without the Riemann hypothesis (node N11)

**Claim.** Breuillard–Varjú's Theorem 2 (Acta Math. 223 (2019), arXiv 1810.13360), proved there under RH for Dedekind zeta functions, holds without any hypothesis on zeros, both parts, with the same rates up to absolute constants, using only Cureton–Thorner's Lean-checked theorem that ζ_K(s) ≠ 0 for Re s > 31/32 for every number field K: for independent coefficients bounded by e^{d^{1/10}}, the middle ones i.i.d. with a law μ with ‖μ‖₂² < 1 − τ, and dτ⁴ ≥ C(log d)^{12} (for every fixed τ once d is large; part 1 already for dτ⁴ ≥ C(log d)²), P = Φ·P̃ with P̃ irreducible (Φ a power of x times cyclotomic factors, deg Φ ≤ (C/τ)√d) with probability ≥ 1 − e^{−cτ√d/log d}, and Gal(P̃) ⊇ Alt(deg P̃) with probability ≥ 1 − e^{−cτ√d/(log d)²}. Consequences, unconditional:
- random ±1 (Littlewood) polynomials of degree n are irreducible with probability → 1 (1 − O(1/n) for n even, 1 − 2√(2/(πn)) + O(1/n) for n odd), with Galois group ⊇ A_n with probability → 1; for every finite-variance law not a point mass, P(irreducible) = 1 − P(a root in the roots of unity of order ≤ N) + O(d^{−N/2}) (BV Cor. 3);
- Eberhard's theorem (2008.01223, Thm 1.3): the characteristic polynomial of a random integer matrix is irreducible with probability 1 − O(e^{−cn}), with Galois group ⊇ A_n with probability 1 − O(e^{−cn^{1/2}});
- Ferber–Jain–Sah–Sawhney (2106.04049, Thm 1.1): the characteristic polynomial of a random symmetric ±1 matrix is irreducible with probability ≥ 1 − 2e^{−cn^{1/4}};
- Varjú–Xu 2511.04240 Thm 1.1 (random multiplicative ±1 coefficients) and its Legendre-symbol corollary in the range f(d) > 2^{7.9π(d)} (with Bebel's Dirichlet half-plane; 2^{32π(d)}d^{65} from Cureton–Thorner alone); Bary-Soroker–Garzoni–Sodin 2502.17218 (tridiagonal matrices); Blachar–Breuillard–Kozma 2609.40123's general-law remark.

**Before.** All of these were proved under RH or ERH only. Unconditionally: Bary-Soroker–Koukoulopoulos–Kozma (Invent. Math. 2023) for laws nearly uniform modulo four primes (e.g. ≥ 35 consecutive integers; rate improved by Bazin, HAL 2025, modulo twelve primes); Bary-Soroker–Hokken–Kozma–Poonen (2308.04878, Thm thm:short): only lim sup P(±1 polynomial irreducible) = 1, the limit "known to hold under GRH"; Kogler (2609.26771, Sep 2026): the 0/1 model, P(reducible) = √(2/(πn)) + O(1/n), by other means, with general coefficient laws announced as future work. Three October 2026 papers still call Eberhard's and FJSS's theorems ERH-conditional.

**Method.** BV use RH only in Prop. 9 (TeX l. 650–698: |ψ_K(x) − x| ≤ C√x(log x log Δ_K + n_K log² x)), which feeds Prop. 19 (l. 1125–1231) and the proof of Thm 2 (l. 2433, 2464–2467). Replacement: BV's own explicit formula smoothed by a box kernel of width e^{−X/32}/4, Lagarias–Odlyzko's zero count and the strip give the same statement with error e^{−X/32}(1 + X)(log Δ_K + n_K(1 + X)) in place of e^{−X/2}(…), with an absolute constant. BV choose X of size τ√d/log d (a power of d), inside their random-walk window (l. 1980–1983), so a fixed-width strip suffices; BV note (l. 548–551) that the classical zero-free region would need primes of size e^{Cd log d}. The descendants use RH only through the same prime sums (Eberhard l. 604–608, 695–700: X = cn/2 → 32cn/33; FJSS l. 481–487, 514–521: X = cn^{1/4}; VX X = 5 log d → 81 log d).
