# Least prime primitive root of every prime without GRH, with numbers (node N11)

**Claim.** Assume H: ζ(s) and every Dirichlet L(s, χ) are zero-free on Re s > B = 0.8733819666132076 (a theorem: Bebel's Lean-kernel-checked Re s > B₂ + 2.2·10⁻²⁵⁴, B₂ = (777 − √1081)/852 < B; TREE.md N7). Let g*(p) be the least prime that is a primitive root mod p, ω = ω(p − 1), 1/(1 − B) = 7.8978.
- Theorem A: for every ω ≤ 10⁴ and every prime p with ω(p − 1) = ω, g*(p) ≤ (κ_ω log p)^{1/(1−B)}, with κ₁ = 3.53 (p − 1 a power of 2), κ₂ = 11.1, κ₅ = 54.1, κ₁₀ = 216, κ₁₀₀ = 2.72·10⁴, κ₁₀₀₀ = 4.32·10⁶, κ₁₀⁴ = 6.85·10⁸ (all values in the certificate's kappa table).
- Theorem B′: every prime p ≥ 5 with ω(p − 1) ≤ 10⁴ (in particular every p ≥ 5 below e^{104403}) has **g*(p) ≤ (0.814 log p)^{3/(1−B)} ≤ (0.814 log p)^{23.70}**.
- Theorem C: if ω(p − 1) > 10⁴, g*(p) ≤ (1.955 log p)^{3.4/(1−B)}.
- Theorem D: every prime p ≥ 3 has **g*(p) ≤ (1.956 log p)^{26.86}**.
- Corollary E: g*(p) < √p − 2 for every prime p ≥ 10^{97.547} (the first Grosswald-type statement for g*(p) without GRH) and g*(p) < p for p ≥ 10^{41.032}.

Sizes: at p ≈ 10^{100} the bounds are 10^{49.0} (worst ω, Theorem A), 10^{53.9} (B′), 10^{71.3} (D); at p ≈ 10^{10}: 10^{29.2}, 10^{30.2}, 10^{44.4}. True values are tiny (g*(p) ≤ 211 for p ≤ 10⁷).

**Before.** Without GRH no bound below p was known for g*(p): "It is not known whether ĝ(p) < p for all p, or even for all sufficiently large p" (Cohen–Trudgian, arXiv 1602.02440). Under GRH: ĝ(p) ≤ ((8/5)(2^{ω(p−1)} − 1) log p)² for p ≥ 10⁹ (McGown–Treviño–Trudgian, arXiv 1508.05182); Shoup/Martin g*(p) ≪ (ω log ω)⁴ log² p without constants. From a zero-free rectangle: Martin, Acta Arith. 80 (1997), Prop. 3, without constants. October 2026 Zenodo notes (23341593, 23352868, 23394711, 23405184, 23423890, 23621868) apply Martin to OpenAI's 7/8 half-plane: g*(p) ≪ (log p)^{24}, "No numerical value of C is asserted"; Zenodo 23623384: g*(p) < p for p ≥ 10^{41} (QRH-conditional, plain sieve). For the least primitive root g(p) (not prime): g(p) < √p − 2 for p > 1.77·10^{54} (arXiv 2608.20431).

**Method.** (1) Detection with Λ(m)w(m/x) over prime powers m (a generating prime power has a generating prime base) and the per-character prime-sum bound of our explicit Linnik theorem ([ExplProg](../Riemann/Deep/ExplProg/), [page](linnik-numeric.md)); every character mod a prime is primitive. (2) The shifted sieve (Iwaniec 1978, as used by Martin; re-derived): lower-bound weights on the first ω primes transport to the actual prime factors of p − 1 with the same main term T_ω and cost C_ω, so the worst case is p − 1 = P_ω·k and each ω is one finite sieve problem. (3) ω ≤ 10⁴: Rosser's linear-sieve weights enumerated exactly (main terms as exact integers in units 2⁻⁶⁴), the best of 79 levels per ω, then one Arb inequality per ω at L = log(P_ω + 1), extended to all larger L with frozen kernel constants and supremum majorants. (4) ω > 10⁴: Buchstab with Selberg's Λ² upper bounds at level (z^{2.3}/q)^{1/2}, three lower bounds for Selberg's G (log min(ξ, q); inclusion–exclusion with Rosser–Schoenfeld; Rankin), 205 cells in log z ∈ [11.56, 60] plus a decreasing normalised tail for log z ≥ 60, giving T ≥ 0.101433·Π_{p≤z}(1 − 1/p) for every z ≥ 104743. (5) p ≤ 10⁷: exact g*(p) for all 664,578 primes.
