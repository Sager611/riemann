# Unconditional root-discriminant bounds beyond the Odlyzko–Poitou limits (nodes N12, N7)

**Claim.** Every number field K of degree n with r₁ real and r₂ complex places satisfies

rd_K = |d_K|^{1/n} ≥ (65.6439 − o(1))^{r₁/n} · (23.3878 − o(1))^{2r₂/n}  (n → ∞),

against the classical unconditional limits 4πe^{1+γ} = 60.8395 and 4πe^γ = 22.3816 (Odlyzko 1976, Poitou 1977). Explicitly, rd_K > 22.38169 for every K of degree n ≥ 2732 and rd_K > 60.84021 for every totally real K of degree n ≥ 1618, so a field with rd_K ≤ 4πe^γ has degree < 2732 (classically no such degree bound); rd_K > 22 for every K of degree ≥ 1581 (Odlyzko's 1976 table reaches 22 only at n = 8862) and rd_K > 60 for every totally real K of degree ≥ 1223. A certified table covers n = 2 … 10⁶ and, for n ≤ 12, every signature. Only finitely many fields have rd_K < 23.3878, and only finitely many totally real fields have rd_K < 65.6439. For abelian K (Bebel's half-plane) the limits are 26.9662 and 84.2999, but for large n the elementary conductor count is stronger there.

**Before.** Odlyzko (1976 tables) and Poitou (1977) give the unconditional limits 22.3816 and 60.8395 from positivity of every zero's contribution when the zeros may fill the whole critical strip; under GRH 44.7632 and 215.3325. These limits had not moved since 1977. No paper derives discriminant bounds from a zero-free strip (novelty below).

**Method.** Poitou's explicit formula with kernels F(x) = S(x/b)·sech(wx), where S is one of Odlyzko's two 1976 shapes and every zero satisfies |Re ρ − ½| ≤ w. F ≥ 0 lets the prime terms be dropped, and every zero still contributes Re Φ(ρ) ≥ 0, because cosh(ax)/cosh(wx) has a positive Fourier transform for |a| ≤ w (Lemma A). Cureton–Thorner's Lean-checked theorem (ζ_K(s) ≠ 0 for Re s > 31/32, every K) gives w = 15/32; the classical case is w = ½ and GRH is w = 0. Within this admissible kernel class sech(wx) is extremal (F(x)·cosh(wx) ≤ 1), which yields the asymptotic constants A_c(w) = exp(γ + log 8π − I_c(w)) and A_r(w) = A_c(w)·exp(π/2 − I_r(w)), with I_c, I_r the integrals of (1 − sech wx)/(2 sinh(x/2)) and (1 − sech wx)/(2 cosh(x/2)). The brief's other idea, kernels with mass on exponents s ≤ 1, fails: −ζ_K'/ζ_K(s) cancels the apparent pole gain.

**Caveat.** A direct substitution of a 2026 input into a 1977 method; the new content is the numbers and the extremal kernel for general w.
